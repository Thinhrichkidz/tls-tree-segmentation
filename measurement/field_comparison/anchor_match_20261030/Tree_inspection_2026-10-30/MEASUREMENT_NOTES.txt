Scope and evidence
The seven current PLYs were read from CloudCompare_review_v2 in the order
074, 163, 103, 079, 069, 132, 139. This completes the six requested checks,
including both alternatives in the final check. Original clouds, project BIN,
existing measurements and field-tag assignments were not saved over or edited.
CloudCompare was used for native viewing and rotation. Detailed inspection
sheets and measurements were also generated directly from the same point data.
source_manifest.csv records exact input filenames, point counts and SHA-256s.

Coordinates and distances
Original coordinates are EPSG:32756 metres per the source metadata. Directions
refer to grid north/east. Horizontal distance = sqrt(deltaE² + deltaN²); this is
not the 3D slope distance. A canopy centroid was never used as a trunk centre.
Saved pipeline feet on leaning trees do not necessarily coincide with the base.
For 074, 163, 103 and both 079 stems, provisional base centres were estimated
from successive lower-bark section centres and their centreline intersection
with the existing terrain mesh. Bark near the ground is partly missing; these
are fitted estimates, not directly observed centres. Alternative height windows
move these fits by about 4–10 cm, but this is only internal sensitivity. Terrain,
base definition and bark coverage introduce larger uncertainty (several dm).
On 069 and 132, ellipse extrapolation was unstable and rejected; their saved
feet are retained. Spacing_measurements.csv preserves both saved-foot distances
and provisional-base distances. Its decimals are for auditability, not accuracy.
Final plan distances were calculated from fitted/saved coordinates, then
verified with CloudCompare two-point picking in north-up top view on explicitly
derived centre markers. Picked distances: 074–103 15.612823 m; 074–163
4.015814 m; 074–079 nearer stem 14.344687 m; 074–069 11.411543 m.
PLAN_CENTRES_ESTIMATED_Z0.ply contains DERIVED centres, not observed scan points,
projected to Z=0 so the picked distance equals horizontal distance. Its CSV maps
point indices to tree IDs. This verifies the distance arithmetic, not the base
location accuracy. Raw bark picks would measure edges rather than centres.

Trunk widths and breast-height convention
Inspection sheets show 10 cm thick horizontal slices at the labelled heights,
referenced to the saved ground elevation. Key clean rings were fitted as
ellipses at 1.2, 1.3 and 1.4 m, with an extra section perpendicular to the local
stem axis. For the strongly leaning trees, a horizontal ellipse's long axis is
not a DBH. A provisional check 1.3 m along the fitted stem from its terrain
intersection gives consistent broad estimates: 074 about 62 cm; 163 about
83 cm; 103 about 78 cm; 079 southern about 77 cm and northern about 69 cm;
139 about 46 cm. These are rounded scan estimates and depend on foot/ground.
No measurement is field-validated. The first report's 1.3 m vertical bands and
the 1.3 m along-stem sections are explicitly distinguished here.
For an oval section, area-equivalent diameter = sqrt(major × minor).
For two separate stems, combined basal-area equivalent = sqrt(d1² + d2²).
This is a comparison statistic; it is not the physical span or one-tree DBH.

Tree 079 discrepancy
The earlier ellipse_key_trees.csv gives about 64 and 63 cm. The current complete
southern ring is about 94 × 75 cm in a horizontal 1.3 m slice; its perpendicular
section is approximately 89 × 73 cm (area equivalent about 81 cm). The northern
ring is about 92 × 64 cm horizontally and 68 × 62 cm perpendicularly (about
65 cm equivalent). Along-stem checking gives about 77 and 69 cm, respectively.
The earlier script splits rings using grid components and trims using circular
radius even when a ring is elliptical. Such trimming can select an incomplete
ring and alter the dimensions. The current result merges both visible arcs of
the southern ring and checks the perpendicular section. Treat 079 width as
approximate (about 80 and 65–70 cm), and do not turn it into a confirmed tag.
The two fitted bases are about 2.86 m apart. They remain visibly separated at
0.1 and 0.6 m and at 1.3 m; no union below breast height is visible. Roots below
the scan surface cannot be judged.

Tree 069 neighbour search
All 163 saved stem positions were checked for neighbours within 5 m. Only
tree_079 is a plausible larger near neighbour: saved distance 3.031 m.
The next saved stems are 152 at 4.62 m and 146 at 4.69 m, both much smaller.
The 079 southern stem is about 2.98 m using its fitted base; 069 base uncertainty
and rounding prevent a strict within-3-m claim. No other larger segmented trunk
was found within the radius; unsegmented or missing stems cannot be excluded.
