1 About Postgraduate Research Project

1.1 An Introduction to this Subject

This project-based subject provides students with opportunities to develop comprehensive research and project
management skills. Students are matched with a supervisor to explore an area of research interest. Working closely
with their supervisor, each student will identify their individual research topic then engage in theoretical and practical
research investigations. Each student is expected to develop their skills to work independently as a researcher to
manage their research project as well as produce and deliver a comprehensive research report on their findings. This
subject provides students with the skills necessary to move on to higher academic research levels or pursue career
opportunities in industry-based research.

1.2 What is Expected of You

Study Load

A student is expected to study an hour per credit point a week. For example a 10 credit point subject would require
10 hours of study per week. This time includes the time spent within classes during lectures, tutorials or practicals.

  Note for Summer Terms: As Summer subjects deliver the same content and classes over a shorter period of time,
the subjects are run in a more intensive mode. Regardless of the delivery mode, the study hours for each subject in
Summer will be around 30 hours.

Attendance

Students must participate in the regular meetings with their supervisor(s).

Approach to Learning

Approach to Learning

INFO6003 Postgraduate Project is a one-semester, problem-based research project subject. Students undertake
an independent research or applied project under the guidance of an academic supervisor, applying the knowledge
and skills developed throughout their postgraduate coursework. The learning approach includes workshops, learning
sessions for the research project, regular consultation with academic supervisors, and other activities (e.g., literature
review, system design and implementation, data collection and analysis, experimentation, evaluation, and technical
writing) necessary to complete the research project. Workshops Multiple workshops will be offered throughout the
semester to support students in developing the practical skills required to undertake and complete their research
projects. These workshops are designed to complement students' independent project work and are not individually
assessed. The workshops include a High Performance Computing (HPC) workshop in Week 3, introducing students to
the University's HPC resources and computational research environment, and a Presentation and LaTeX workshop in
Week 4, focusing on effective research presentations, academic writing, LaTeX document preparation, and reference
management. Additional workshops or research seminars may be provided where appropriate. Consultation with
Academic Supervisor(s) Students will carry out their research projects in consultation with their academic supervisor(s).
Students are expected to attend regular meetings, normally on a weekly basis or as otherwise agreed with their
supervisor(s), to discuss project progress, research challenges, experimental results, and future work.

To ensure a successful start to the project, students are advised to meet with their supervisor(s) during the first
week of the semester to discuss the project scope, expectations, and an initial project plan. Students are required
to submit a Supervisor Agreement by Week 3 and a Project Proposal by Week 4. Given the condensed timeline of
a one-semester project, early engagement with the supervisor and timely commencement of research activities are

essential. Delays in starting the project may significantly impact a student's ability to complete the required project
outcomes.

Importantly:

It is the student's responsibility to arrange regular meetings with their supervisor(s) and seek advice on project progress
throughout the semester.

Students are expected to attend scheduled research meetings at the agreed time. Failure to maintain regular
communication and satisfactory progress may adversely affect the successful completion of the project.

Students are required to maintain appropriate records of project work, data, software development, experimental
results, and research activities. Supervisors may require students to maintain an electronic project notebook or a
GitHub repository to facilitate project supervision and assessment.

For communication, it is expected that students respond to supervisor emails and other communications in a timely
and professional manner. Learning Sessions This is a 10 credit point subject. Students are expected to spend
approximately 10 hours per week on project-related activities. These activities include independent research, literature
review, project implementation, experimentation, meetings with supervisors, attendance at workshops, report writing,
and preparation for the final presentation.

3.1 Subject Learning Outcomes

   Outcome

1 Critically analyse the relevant literature to identify potential research problems.

2 Generate research questions and hypothesis based on literature review.

3 Justify the research proposal in relation to its significance in literature and its anticipated impact.

4 Construct a plan and methodology to conduct research on an identified question/issue/problem.

5 Articulate research aims and findings in professional, formal and informal formats and contexts.

6 Apply self-management skills in planning and executing research.

7  Demonstrate research ethics in synthesising complex information from a range of sources and referencing

Academic Integrity and Student Misconduct Rule

 Western cares about your success as a student and in your future career. Studying with academic integrity safeguards
your professional reputation and your degree. All Western students must:

    ­ be familiar with the policies listed below;
    ­ apply principles of academic integrity;
    ­ act honestly and ethically in producing all academic work and assessment tasks; and
    ­ submit work that is their own and acknowledge any sources used in their work.

Each time you submit an assessment, you will declare that you have completed it individually, unless it is a group
assignment. In the case of a group assignment, each group member should be ready to document their individual
contribution if needed.

The Student Misconduct Rule applies to all students of Western Sydney University including Western Sydney
University programs taught by other education providers. You must not engage in academic, research or general
misconduct as defined in the Rule or you may be subject to sanctions. The University considers submitting falsified
documentation in support of requests to redo, resit or extend submissions, including sitting of deferred examinations,
as instances of general misconduct.

More information is available in the Academic Integrity Guidelines. It is your responsibility to apply these principles
to all work you submit to the University.

Special Consideration and Requests for Extensions

Western recognises that there may be times when things outside of your control impact your ability to complete your
studies.

You can submit a Special Consideration or extension application to request that you are:

If you do not have documentation at the time of submitting your application, you should still apply. You may be
asked to provide supporting documents at a later stage.

Marking Criteria:

Project Definition and Motivation (30%)

F (<49%): Project topic is unclear, poorly motivated, or not feasible for a one-semester project. Aims or research
questions are missing or inappropriate.
P (50-65%): Project topic and aims are identified, but motivation, scope, or research questions lack clarity or depth.
Feasibility is only partly addressed.
C (65-74%): Project is clearly defined with appropriate motivation, aims, and scope. Feasibility within one semester
is generally demonstrated.
D (75-84%): Project is well motivated, clearly scoped, and supported by strong aims or research questions. Feasibility
and project value are clearly justified.
HD (85-100%): Project is highly coherent, well motivated, and clearly significant. Aims, scope, research questions,
and feasibility are exceptionally well articulated.

Background and Literature (25%)

F (<49%): Background is missing, inaccurate, or unsupported by relevant literature.
P (50-65%): Provides basic background and some relevant sources, but coverage is limited or mostly descriptive.
C (65-74%): Provides a clear background and discusses relevant literature appropriately, identifying the project
context and need.
D (75-84%): Demonstrates strong understanding of relevant literature, with clear connections to the proposed
project and its contribution.
HD (85-100%): Demonstrates excellent command of the literature, critically identifying gaps, limitations, and a
strong rationale for the proposed work.

Proposed Methodology (25%)

F (<49%): Methodology is missing, vague, inappropriate, or not aligned with the project aims.
P (50-65%): Methodology is described at a basic level but lacks detail, justification, or clear alignment with the
project objectives.
C (65-74%): Methodology is appropriate, clearly described, and generally aligned with the project aims.
D (75-84%): Methodology is well developed, justified, and feasible. Key methods, data, tools, evaluation plans, or
implementation steps are clearly explained.
HD (85-100%): Methodology is rigorous, well justified, and highly appropriate. Proposed methods, evaluation
strategy, resources, and implementation plan demonstrate strong technical maturity.

Project Plan and Feasibility (20%)

F (<49%): Project plan is missing, unrealistic, or does not demonstrate how the project can be completed within
the semester.
P (50-65%): Provides a basic project plan or timeline, but milestones, risks, resources, or feasibility are only partly
addressed.
C (65-74%): Provides a clear and realistic project plan with appropriate milestones and consideration of feasibility.
D (75-84%): Provides a well-structured and realistic plan with clear milestones, resource considerations, and
awareness of risks or constraints.
HD (85-100%): Provides an excellent, detailed, and highly feasible project plan. Milestones, risks, resources, ethics
or data requirements, and expected outcomes are carefully considered.

Instructions:

 Report

Length: 12 pages in LNCS format or 7 pages in an approved machine learning conference format, including references.

The final report assessment consists of two equally important components: the written report and the project's
GitHub repository. Each component contributes 50% of the marks for this assessment.

The written report is the major written component of the project and should present a complete account of the
work undertaken throughout the semester. The report should clearly explain the project motivation, objectives,
methodology, implementation, experimental evaluation, results, discussion and conclusions. Students are expected
to demonstrate critical thinking, technical competence and sound analytical reasoning. The report should be clearly
written, logically organised and presented according to accepted academic standards. Report templates will be made
available in Ultra.

The GitHub repository is assessed as evidence of the project's development process and technical implementation.
Students are expected to maintain the repository throughout the semester, making regular commits that document
the evolution of the project. As a general expectation, students should commit meaningful progress on at least a
weekly basis. Assessment will consider the quality and organisation of the repository, code quality, documentation,
reproducibility, version control practices, and the consistency of development over time. Repositories that are largely
populated only at the end of the semester, with little evidence of continuous development, will receive substantially
lower marks regardless of the quality of the final code.

Marking Criteria:

Written Report (50%)

F (<49%): Report is incomplete, poorly organised, or lacks essential sections. Methods, results, or conclusions are
missing or inadequately explained. Writing quality is poor and does not meet postgraduate academic standards.
P (50-65%): Report covers the main components of the project but lacks depth or clarity in several areas. Some
methodology, analysis, or discussion is underdeveloped. Writing and organisation are generally acceptable.
C (65-74%): Well-structured report that clearly presents the project motivation, methodology, implementation,
results, discussion and conclusions. Writing is clear and demonstrates sound academic practice with only minor
weaknesses.
D (75-84%): High-quality report with well-justified methodology, comprehensive analysis and thoughtful discussion.
Writing is professional, logically organised and demonstrates strong critical thinking.
HD (85-100%): Outstanding report of publication quality. Demonstrates excellent technical writing, critical analysis,
rigorous evaluation and insightful discussion. The report is exceptionally clear, coherent and professionally presented.

GitHub Repository (30%)

F (<49%): Repository is missing, poorly organised, largely empty, or contains only a final upload with little evidence
of development throughout the semester. Documentation is absent or inadequate.
P (50-65%): Repository contains the project code and basic documentation but shows limited evidence of continuous
development. Commit history is inconsistent or infrequent.
C (65-74%): Repository is organised and documented with regular commits demonstrating ongoing development
during the semester. Code is generally reproducible and appropriately managed using version control.
D (75-84%): Repository is well organised, clearly documented and demonstrates consistent development through
meaningful commits made throughout the semester. Good software engineering and version control practices are
evident.
HD (85-100%): Repository demonstrates exemplary software engineering practice. Development is consistently
documented through regular, meaningful commits across the semester. Code is clean, reproducible, well documented
and professionally maintained.

Technical Quality and Analysis (20%)

F (<49%): Technical work is incomplete or incorrect. Experiments, evaluation or analysis are missing or poorly
executed. Conclusions are unsupported.
P (50-65%): Technical implementation is functional but limited. Evaluation is basic and analysis lacks depth. Some
conclusions are only partially supported by evidence.
C (65-74%): Sound technical implementation with appropriate experimentation and evaluation. Analysis is generally
accurate and conclusions are supported by the results.
D (75-84%): Strong technical implementation with comprehensive evaluation, thoughtful analysis and well-supported
conclusions. Demonstrates good critical reasoning and awareness of limitations.
HD (85-100%): Excellent technical work demonstrating innovation, rigorous experimentation, comprehensive
evaluation and insightful analysis. Conclusions are thoroughly justified and reflect a high level of technical maturity.

Instructions:

 Duration: 15 minutes for an individual project or 20 minutes for a pair project, followed by questions.

Presentations are strictly limited to the allocated time. Students are required to attend and participate in the entire
presentation session.

The final presentation provides students with the opportunity to communicate the motivation, objectives, methodology,
implementation, key results and conclusions of their project. Students should demonstrate a thorough understanding
of the work undertaken, justify the technical decisions made throughout the project, discuss the significance and
limitations of their findings, and reflect on possible future work where appropriate. Students should also be prepared
to respond to questions from the academic panel and audience.

Assessment will consider the quality and technical depth of the project, clarity of communication, presentation skills,
organisation, ability to answer questions, and overall understanding of the project.

Presentation slides are intended to support the presentation rather than serve as a standalone document. Students
are encouraged to use concise slides that communicate key ideas clearly, with appropriate use of figures, diagrams
and demonstrations where relevant.

Students who do not participate in the entire presentation session will receive an FNS (Fail - Non Submission) grade
for the subject. Delivering an individual presentation alone does not satisfy this requirement.
